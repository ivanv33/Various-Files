# Decomposition Frameworks

Twenty-two decomposition frameworks documented as knowledge-graph schemas. Each one has a folder with a README (the framework, two worked examples, and how to build a knowledge graph with it), a `graph.json` (the examples as data), and a self-contained Three.js page that renders the examples as an interactive graph. Open [`index.html`](./index.html) for the overview, or pick a framework from the table below.

The one question every page answers: **when text is turned into a graph using this framework as the schema, which nodes and edges come straight from the data, and which must an LLM derive?**

## Why

The frameworks will later be run over the TBPN transcript corpus in this repository (`../tbpn-transcripts/`, 252 episodes plus per-episode LLM extractions) to generate ideas and to identify business opportunities, underserved markets and startup ideas. For that to be trustworthy, every graph has to keep the line between evidence and reasoning visible. So every example here is grounded twice: a classic textbook case, and a real moment from the transcripts with quotes that the validator checks against the file.

## Provenance in one paragraph

Every node and every edge is `fact` or `derived`. A fact is stated in the source and carries a verbatim quote and a reference. A derived item is LLM reasoning the source does not contain and carries a confidence and a rationale. Derived nodes point at the facts that support them through `supported_by` edges. Framework scaffolding that is neither (the six Ishikawa categories, the Cynefin domains) is marked `schema` and drawn grey. In every graph page the "LLM-derived" toggle hides the reasoning layer, and what remains must still be true to the source. The full rules are in [SPEC.md](./SPEC.md) section 3.

## What the graphs derived

One paragraph per framework, each spelling out the same formula: given these facts from one TBPN episode, applying this method, we derived these things, and the opportunity that fell out. Each links to its README and to its graph opened in Insights mode.

**[STAR / PAR](01-narrative-and-statement/star-par/README.md)** · [graph](01-narrative-and-statement/star-par/index.html?insights=1)

Given that interim CEO Jeff Wang and Cognition CEO Scott Wu told TBPN how Windsurf, its founders hired away by Google DeepMind and rivals circling the go-to-market team left behind, was sold to Cognition over one weekend, applying STAR / PAR we derived that Wang's real objective was to find the whole company a home before its sales staff took those offers, that closing within days is what kept that team from leaving, and that the problem was a company with product, customers and a commercial team but no founders and no time; the opportunity that falls out is a two-sided market: engineer-heavy AI labs buying a commercial organisation whole, and default protection for employees a founder-only hire strands.

**[CARL](01-narrative-and-statement/carl/README.md)** · [graph](01-narrative-and-statement/carl/index.html?insights=1)

Given Ylan Richard's account on TBPN of closing Cala, whose robotic restaurants were profitable with proven payback yet died when Europe's Series A to B funding cliff blocked scaling and French severance law priced the restructure out, applying CARL we derived that no single-loop fix could have saved it, that regulation made a solvable cost problem unsolvable, and that jurisdiction, financing and ownership are design variables chosen at incorporation; the opportunities are a formation tool pricing wind-down cost, and a growth vehicle for proven physical businesses across Europe's A to B gap.

**[Minto SCQA](01-narrative-and-statement/minto-scqa/README.md)** · [graph](01-narrative-and-statement/minto-scqa/index.html?insights=1)

Given that Poseidon founder David Zagaynov told TBPN that logistics has gone a hundred years unchanged, that the Soviet ekranoplan's old blockers are now solved, and that ground-effect craft are certified by the Coast Guard rather than the FAA, so what he is building are flying boats, applying Minto SCQA we derived the question the pitch never asks (the cheapest way to move coastal and island freight by a boat-certified craft), the unstated design rule (let the certification boundary set the specification) and the constraint's move from physics to paperwork; the opportunity that falls out is the booking, insurance and dockside handling Poseidon is not building, and a screen for hard-tech markets a cheaper certifier already governs.

**[Pyramid Principle](01-narrative-and-statement/pyramid-principle/README.md)** · [graph](01-narrative-and-statement/pyramid-principle/index.html?insights=1)

Given Elad Gil, on TBPN in October 2025, naming three underappreciated things about the AI wave (capability one API call away, markets like legal that never bought software now open, markets shifting from seats to labour) and only afterwards saying people will miss their size, applying the Pyramid Principle we derived the unstated governing thought: AI markets sized with software instincts will be measured wrong; a fourth pillar he left aside: process change sets the rate, so the market is large and slow; and the rule behind legal: open markets have no software line item and a large payroll. The opportunity: pricing AI against displaced labour rather than seats, and selling the deployment layer as a product rather than consulting.

**[Toulmin Model](01-narrative-and-statement/toulmin-model/README.md)** · [graph](01-narrative-and-statement/toulmin-model/index.html?insights=1)

Given Glenn Hutchins's case, on the TBPN bubbles episode, that the AI data-center build-out is not the dot-com CLEC overbuild because almost every data center has a solvent counterparty contracted for all its output, returns about 2x over a four-to-five-year deal and leaves an option on the used GPUs, applying the Toulmin Model we derived the rule he never states, that contracted offtake moves demand risk onto the buyer's balance sheet; a qualifier he does not draw, that it weakens for capital-burning labs; and two rebuttals nobody connected on air, weak offtaker credit and used GPUs losing value. The opportunities are a counterparty-risk rating or insurance product for compute offtake contracts, and a priced residual-value benchmark for used accelerators.

**[Dialectical Decomposition](01-narrative-and-statement/dialectical-decomposition/README.md)** · [graph](01-narrative-and-statement/dialectical-decomposition/index.html?insights=1)

Given TBPN's staged Slop-versus-Steel debate, in which Everett Randle of Kleiner Perkins argued that a digital product's form factor and distribution lend themselves to accumulating power, and Delian Asparouhov of Founders Fund argued that gross margin is the wrong metric because terminal EBITDA follows monopoly, applying Dialectical Decomposition we derived that the real contest is what makes a business costly to copy, that the atoms-versus-bits axis is one neither side believes, and that durable pricing power belongs to whoever controls the scarcest input at their layer; the opportunity that falls out is margin-aware inference routing tied to an application's own pricing, and a diligence product that scores rebuild cost and switching cost instead of day-one gross margin.

**[MECE Principle](02-strategic-and-business/mece/README.md)** · [graph](02-strategic-and-business/mece/index.html?insights=1)

Given that Elad Gil, asked on TBPN on 10 October 2025 about AI companies in vertical markets, named the finalists in foundation models, coding and healthcare, called financial tooling, sales enablement and accounting crowded with no winner, and sized the services economy AI can enter at about $5 trillion, applying the MECE Principle we derived that the labs sit inside every vertical bucket, that the named verticals cover a sliver of the whole, that labor spend sits in no branch, and that a crowded branch is not an empty one; the opportunity that falls out is the service lines nobody can name, screened for near-zero software spend and high payroll, priced as work delivered rather than seats.

**[Issue & Hypothesis Trees](02-strategic-and-business/issue-hypothesis-trees/README.md)** · [graph](02-strategic-and-business/issue-hypothesis-trees/index.html?insights=1)

Given that TBPN's hosts, on the 2025-08-09 recap, relayed the Wall Street Journal's report that Azure grew 39 percent with non-AI core infrastructure as the driver and volunteered five explanations for why infrastructure is outgrowing tokens-as-a-service, applying Issue & Hypothesis Trees we derived that two of those explanations really say the AI versus non-AI split is mismeasured, that a demand-side branch exists which nobody on air opened, and that four of the five came without the observation that would kill them; the opportunity that falls out is a workload-attribution layer reporting a defensible AI share from usage telemetry, and deployment tooling for the gap between announced enterprise AI programmes and inference in production.

**[5 Whys](02-strategic-and-business/five-whys/README.md)** · [graph](02-strategic-and-business/five-whys/index.html?insights=1)

Given Brian Armstrong's Crypto Day account of the Coinbase breach, where attackers bribed overseas support contractors for customer data, phished a handful of customers and demanded $20 million, applying 5 Whys we derived that agents hold standing access to identity data so a bribe buys a lookup, that the root cause is data worth far more than its holder's wage in a 24/7 outsourced function, and that the 60% AI resolution rate is a security control; the opportunity is a PII-redacting agent console, AI support sold as an insider-threat control, and continuous vendor-access attestation.

**[Ishikawa (Fishbone) Diagram](02-strategic-and-business/ishikawa-fishbone/README.md)** · [graph](02-strategic-and-business/ishikawa-fishbone/index.html?insights=1)

Given that Chris Power of Hadrian, on TBPN's Defense Tech Day, said the United States has no industrial base left and named a cause on every front - machinists aging out, 1950s plant, no domestic CNC makers, CAM tools that emit bad code, no public process data, offshoring, export control - applying the Ishikawa diagram we derived that the missing factories and the missing workforce keep each other missing, that the process data is not absent but locked inside running factories, and that nobody sells a retrofit for the old plant; the opportunities are a retrofit layer for computerless machine tools, a domestic machine-tool builder or allied-equipment leasing vehicle, and the instrumented operation as the moat in export-controlled verticals.

**[Rumelt's Strategic Kernel](02-strategic-and-business/rumelt-strategy-kernel/README.md)** · [graph](02-strategic-and-business/rumelt-strategy-kernel/index.html?insights=1)

Given that Max Levchin, interviewed on TBPN about Affirm, said lending is a dirty industry because it is all about late fees and compounding interest, that he was told a lender without them could not survive, and that Affirm was built to have neither and so has less margin, applying Rumelt's Strategic Kernel we derived that the industry's profit pool is the borrower's own error, that renouncing it makes underwriting accuracy the binding constraint, and that the obstacle which outlasts the rest is an asterisk nobody prices; the opportunities that fall out are no-asterisk versions of the products where the borrower's error is largest, and a machine-readable true-cost layer that lets an AI advisor rank lenders by the footnote.

**[Theory of Constraints & Evaporating Cloud](02-strategic-and-business/theory-of-constraints/README.md)** · [graph](02-strategic-and-business/theory-of-constraints/index.html?insights=1)

Given that CoreWeave's Brian Venturo, on TBPN, dismissed power, transformers, UPS and electricians as problems only against a short clock, named speculative capital as the biggest constraint, and said he must either build ahead from his balance sheet and risk the company or haircut demand and arrive still constrained, applying Theory of Constraints and the Evaporating Cloud we derived the unstated beliefs holding that conflict up: only its own equity can fund capacity ahead of contracts, demand risk can only be avoided rather than transferred, and a dollar of capacity is a dollar of risk; the opportunity that falls out is a forward market in GPU-hour contracts underwritten by investment-grade offtakers, and the underwriting layer that makes them financeable.

**[First Principles Thinking](03-engineering-and-cognitive/first-principles/README.md)** · [graph](03-engineering-and-cognitive/first-principles/index.html?insights=1)

Given Casey Handmer's answer on TBPN, asked why solar not nuclear and whether panels belong in orbit, that a reactor is a steam engine costing no less than coal even without the NRC, that an acre earns $500 farming or $100,000 to $200,000 under solar, and that an orbital watt earns a billion times more as internet than as beamed power, applying First Principles Thinking we derived that nuclear's floor is the turbine, beyond licence reform, that the land gap is 200x to 400x, and that orbit's watt goes to the highest bidder; the opportunities are a brokerage for an acre's conversion option, a paid covenant so food can bid for the acre, and revenue per watt as diligence screen.

**[Functional Decomposition](03-engineering-and-cognitive/functional-decomposition/README.md)** · [graph](03-engineering-and-cognitive/functional-decomposition/index.html?insights=1)

Given Crusoe chief executive Chase Lockmiller's 29 May 2025 account of the 1.2-gigawatt Abilene AI factory that turns curtailed Texas wind and solar, fifteen billion of Blue Owl credit and users' prompts into intelligence, naming transformers, switchgear, chillers and a closed water loop, applying Functional Decomposition we derived the box he never names (stepping voltage down to the data hall), the firming guarantee an intermittent source owes a load that cannot blink, the financeable delivery date switchgear buyers cannot buy, and the single-tenant availability promise the debt rests on; the opportunity that falls out is firming sold as a product on a curtailed grid, and lead-time insurance on long-lead electrical gear for developers too small to build their own.

**[Pólya's 4-Step Method](03-engineering-and-cognitive/polya-four-step/README.md)** · [graph](03-engineering-and-cognitive/polya-four-step/index.html?insights=1)

Given that TBPN's host covering the OpenAI-AMD deal found OpenAI committed to six gigawatts of AMD chips, asked researcher Tyler its dollar value, heard 'tens of billions per gigawatt' from AMD and 'around fifty billion' from an analyst, and floated a hundred billion, then four hundred, then 'that's totally wrong', applying Pólya's 4-Step Method we derived that his stated plan (watts per chip, chip count, times price) was never carried out, that the two estimates differ fourfold and cannot both stand, and that AMD's own 2027 guidance bounds the price; the opportunity is a per-gigawatt bill of materials that analysts and lenders now rebuild by hand, and the live spread between credible readings of a deal, sold as a signal.

**[Means-Ends Analysis](03-engineering-and-cognitive/means-ends-analysis/README.md)** · [graph](03-engineering-and-cognitive/means-ends-analysis/index.html?insights=1)

Given Chris Power's account of Hadrian on TBPN (17 July 2025), where revenue grew tenfold, the next factory is four times bigger, a program goes from one aircraft to twenty, and a billion dollars could not hire two thousand welders, applying Means-Ends Analysis we derived that floor space grows four times against demand growing twenty, so the rest must come from output per person; that automation and hiring both require a trained workforce nobody can buy; and that tenfold productivity is gated on shop-floor software not yet written. The opportunity that falls out is a contracted training-and-placement pipeline for machinists, welders and inspectors, and shop-floor software for machining, welding and castings for every factory gated on the same missing layer.

**[Inversion & Pre-Mortem](03-engineering-and-cognitive/inversion-premortem/README.md)** · [graph](03-engineering-and-cognitive/inversion-premortem/index.html?insights=1)

Given that Blake Scholl of Boom Supersonic told TBPN that Superpower, a 42 megawatt gas turbine for data centres, will reach 200 megawatts in 18 months and two gigawatts a year from a second factory, on 300 million dollars and a last-ever equity round, with the cash funding the Overture airliner, applying Inversion & Pre-Mortem we derived that the plan is priced at the good case with no slack, that any turbine slip becomes an airliner slip because the equity door was publicly closed, and that one field failure could freeze the order book as the big factory opens; the opportunities that fall out are a 24-hour superalloy parts service, a delivery-and-uptime bond for unproven vendors, and pre-permitted turbine pads.

**[Cynefin Framework](04-sensemaking-and-complex-systems/cynefin/README.md)** · [graph](04-sensemaking-and-complex-systems/cynefin/index.html?insights=1)

Given TBPN's Flight 10 recap and its reading of David Senra on how Elon Musk attacks cost at SpaceX, setting a fixed Mars window, a $3M cooling quote, a four-bolt spec and a cracked engine skirt side by side, applying the Cynefin Framework we derived that the window is a rule to categorise against, the quote a bundle to unpick, the bolts answerable only by flying two, and the skirt call about decision latency; the opportunities are assurance sold apart from hardware, inherited-requirement auditing, pre-delegated decision authority, and a per-part domain scoreboard for hard-tech diligence.

**[Wardley Mapping](04-sensemaking-and-complex-systems/wardley-mapping/README.md)** · [graph](04-sensemaking-and-complex-systems/wardley-mapping/index.html?insights=1)

Given that Poolside's Eiso Kant told TBPN on 16 October 2025 that frontier intelligence is a commodity sold as tokens behind an API, that his defense customers need it running in air-gapped, ATO-bound sites, and that at 250 megawatts of powered data-centre shell there is no one left to call, applying Wardley Mapping we derived that intelligence is finishing its slide into a metered utility, that the powered shell is the one component stuck at custom-built while demand outruns supply, and that Poolside's truckable skid is a first repeatable unit; the opportunity that falls out is selling factory-built data-hall skids as a standard product, and a vendor-neutral layer taking any frontier model into air-gapped and edge environments.

**[OODA Loop](04-sensemaking-and-complex-systems/ooda-loop/README.md)** · [graph](04-sensemaking-and-complex-systems/ooda-loop/index.html?insights=1)

## Viewing

- `index.html` and every framework page open from the file system. The graph pages load Three.js from jsDelivr, so they need internet access; the graph data is embedded in each page.
- On GitHub the README links work directly; the graph pages need GitHub Pages or a local clone.
- In a graph page: drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, right-drag to pan, hover for a summary, click a node for its quote or rationale. Keys: `d` toggles derived nodes, `g` toggles grounding links, `i` toggles Insights mode, `r` resets the view. URL parameters set the initial state: `?example=1&derived=0&grounding=1&insights=1`.
- **Insights mode** (`i`) is the LLM layer on its own: fact nodes and stated edges ghost to a faint outline, inferences keep full strength, grounding links show, and a card lists every inference by slot with the idea-bearing slot first and the opportunities starred. The card opens with the long form of that example's insight paragraph.

## What the examples showed

Some things only became visible once all 22 were built.

- **Well-told stories are mostly inference.** Under the rule that a connection counts as stated only when one speaker says it, the evidence-only view of a narrative framework is sparse: Minto SCQA's transcript example has 16 quoted facts joined by 4 stated connections, STAR/PAR 20 joined by 7. The structure a listener hears was supplied by the listener. The READMEs treat this as the finding, and for the end goal it is the useful signal: the links that carry an argument from a transcript will mostly be inferences with confidences, not observations.
- **Empty slots say true things.** Rumelt's Diagnosis band is empty for Apple's 1997 plan until inference is switched on; Pólya's Look Back phase is empty for every transcript example; 5 Whys stops at a divider captioned "the source stops here". Each is the framework's own thesis rendered as data.
- **A quote being verbatim does not make its claim true.** First Principles keeps a speaker's "Brayton cycle" for what is in fact a Rankine plant, and uses it as the worked example of a quoted fundamental truth that needs checking rather than copying.
- **The registry under-supplied stated relations.** Four frameworks (Minto SCQA, Inversion & Pre-Mortem, Pólya, Cynefin) each added one relation beyond `_meta/frameworks.json`, all for the same reason: every registry relation for that framework originated at a derived node, so the evidence-only view would have been unconnected quotes. Slot ids are unchanged everywhere. The additions are listed in each folder's `state.json.notes` and README; a merge should treat them as part of the schema.
- **Entity spellings follow the extractions, errors included.** `entities` are spelled as in `tbpn-transcripts/extractions/` because that is the join key for the merge, so "Ripling" and "Everett Randall" stand where the extraction has them, and the README prose gives the real spelling beside it. The merge step should carry a canonical alias next to the join key.

## How it was built, and how to resume

The build is agent-driven and every step is committed. One framework agent per slug runs a scout (finds the transcript moment, with no suggestion from the orchestrator), writes the graph, then a README writer and a viz builder; the viz builder is judged by a fresh, independent critic each iteration until the critic passes (the critic caught one builder misreporting its own metrics); then the folder is reconciled field by field against `graph.json` and committed. Each framework's `state.json` records which steps are done. To resume after an interruption, run `node _meta/status.mjs` and relaunch `_meta/AGENT_BRIEF.md` Part A for any slug that is not `done`; the build survived four interruptions this way without losing committed work. The full contract is [SPEC.md](./SPEC.md).

The shared viewer, `_meta/graph-engine.html`, was fixed twelve times during the build, almost always because a viz builder reported a defect rather than patching its own page. Pages built after the brief said what the engine provides need no page-local code at all; the seven built before it carry only genuinely framework-specific visuals (Pyramid's sloped tiers, CARL's loop arcs, Ishikawa's skeleton).
