# 2019 Lexus GX 460 — Ride Height & Tilt Analysis (from 4 seller photos)

Analysis date: 2026-07-18 · Vehicle: 2019 GX 460 (2014+ facelift front end), ~70k mi, stock 18" split-spoke wheels, highway all-season tires, factory-style hitch receiver, CA car (Bakersfield-area dealer plate frame).

## Verdict (TL;DR)

- **Ride height: stock.** Estimated standing height ≈ 74–76 in vs the factory spec of **74.2 in (6'2")**; fender gaps and body proportions match an unmodified GX 460. No lift, no lowering, no sag. Factory ground clearance applies: **8.1 in**.
- **Side-to-side tilt: none detectable.** Combined front + rear photo analysis gives a body-vs-axle lean of **≈ −0.3° ± 1.1°** — statistically zero. A failed air spring or broken/settled coil (typically a 1.5–2 in corner drop ≈ 2°+) would be unmistakable in these views and is absent.
- **Front-to-rear rake: normal factory stance.** Rear sits ~0.5–1 in taller at the fenders than the front (rear-high when unloaded is how these leave the factory). This specifically argues against leaking rear air springs (Luxury trim) or tired rear coils, which produce the opposite (rear-low squat).

## Method (why these numbers are trustworthy from casual photos)

1. **Camera-roll cancellation.** Phone shots are never level, so absolute angles are meaningless. Instead I compared the car's own body reference line (taillight bases in the rear shot, headlight bases in the front shot) against the line through its tire contact patches *in the same frame*. Camera tilt moves both lines identically; only real suspension lean makes them diverge.
2. **Road-crown immunity.** The truck is parked next to a curb/gutter, where pavement slopes. That tilts body and wheels *together*, so it also cancels in the body-vs-contact comparison.
3. **Per-wheel scale calibration.** Each visible rim face is a known 18 in circle and the stock 265/60R18 tire is 30.5 in OD, giving an inches-per-pixel scale *at that wheel*, which neutralizes perspective/foreshortening in the ¾ shots.
4. **Uncertainty via Monte Carlo.** Every feature location carries a ±4–7 px prior; 200k samples propagate that to the quoted 95% intervals. (Source photos weren't available as files, so feature coordinates are careful visual reads — the error bars reflect that honestly.)

## What this rules out / flags for this specific truck

- **Ruled out (at photo resolution):** collapsed rear air spring (Luxury trim), broken coil, worn-out one-side spring, lift/leveling kit, oversized tires (sidewall-to-rim proportions match 265/60R18).
- **Not determinable from photos:** trim (Premium vs **Luxury — Luxury adds rear auto-leveling air suspension**, a future maintenance item), KDSS presence, shock condition (dampers can be dead with perfect ride height), alignment.
- Factory-style **hitch receiver** is visible — ask about towing history.

## 5-minute in-person verification checklist

1. Park on the flattest slab available, fuel level noted (a full 23 gal tank adds ~140 lb on the **driver** side — up to ~0.4 in of driver-side drop there is *normal* on this platform).
2. Tape-measure **hub center straight up to fender lip** at all four corners. Expect ≈ 19.5–20 in front, 20.5–21 in rear; **left/right should match within ~0.5 in**, rear taller than front by ~0.7–1.2 in.
3. If it's a **Luxury**: let it sit 30+ min off, measure rear, start it, wait, re-measure — the leveling compressor should restore height quickly and without loud/continuous running. Check rear wheel wells for air bags vs coils to confirm trim.
4. Bounce-test each corner (2–3 slow oscillations max) and check tire wear evenness — cupping/inner-edge wear reveals dead shocks or alignment issues that ride height hides.
5. If equipped with KDSS: uneven lean that *appears after* jacking/service is a known KDSS re-set quirk — a dealer can rebalance it; it's not a broken frame.
