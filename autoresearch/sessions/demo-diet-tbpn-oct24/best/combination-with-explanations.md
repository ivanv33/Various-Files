# Multi-Framework Decomposition: Startup Opportunity Engine

This protocol combines **Wardley Mapping**, **Rumelt's Strategic Kernel**, and the **PDCA (Deming) Cycle** to extract underserved startup opportunities from the Diet TBPN October 24, 2025 episode and translate them into concrete, de-risked month-one execution roadmaps for a two-person team.

---

### Step 1: Wardley Mapping (`wardley-mapping`)

#### 1. What it decomposes in this transcript
Wardley Mapping is applied across the core technological dislocations and market debates covered in the episode:
- **Synthetic Influence Operations & Account Swarms**: A16Z-backed Double Speed enabling control of thousands of AI personas to "never pay a human again" vs. the defensive verification and attribution layer required by platforms, brands, and public discourse.
- **Spatial Computing & Ecosystem Standstill**: Samsung Galaxy XR powered by Android XR challenging Apple Vision Pro, highlighting the developer "wait-and-see" standoff (studios demanding 100M DAUs before committing custom native development).
- **In-Home Ambient Health Sensing**: Kohler's optical toilet camera entering the diagnostic space against Throne, exposing consumer trust friction, edge vs. cloud data routing, and clinical integration hurdles.
- **AI Infrastructure & Legal Personhood Boundaries**: TurboPuffer proving first-principles serverless vector/full-text search over commoditized object storage, set against Ohio's legislative push stripping AI agents of personhood, asset ownership, and directorships.

#### 2. What to look for
- **Anchor User & Needs**: Identify the real user experiencing the friction (e.g., the indie XR developer stalled by platform fragmentation; the developer needing cheap, compliant storage/search; the enterprise brand defending against synthetic account swarms).
- **Evolution Axis Placement**: Accurately categorize each component across its evolutionary stage:
  - *Genesis*: Novel frontiers (e.g., autonomous legal agent wrappers under state restrictions, non-invasive optical biomonitoring).
  - *Custom-Built*: Tailored implementations (e.g., bespoke spatial app porting, custom bot-detection heuristics).
  - *Product*: Packaged offerings (e.g., Samsung Galaxy XR, Kohler toilet camera, Double Speed bot tooling).
  - *Commodity/Utility*: Standardized substrates (e.g., cloud object storage, Android XR base OS, commodity consumer battery packs, foundational LLM APIs).
- **Failure Modes to Guard Against**:
  - Do not position components on evolution based on elapsed time or media popularity; evaluate strictly on standardizability and ubiquity.
  - Avoid inventing components unsupported by the transcript text (e.g., do not extrapolate hypothetical hardware specs beyond what the hosts discuss).
  - Ensure every value chain anchors on a defined user need rather than a floating technology category.
- **Whitespace Identification**: Pinpoint components undergoing rapid commoditization that leave upstream or downstream bottlenecks unserved—especially where heavy incumbents (Oracle, Apple, Kohler) over-index on capital intensity, leaving agile software middleware unaddressed.

#### 3. What to hand to the next framework
Pass 2 to 3 high-potential structural whitespace areas where evolutionary shifts create an urgent operational gap suitable for an agile, low-overhead software team.

### Step 2: Rumelt's Strategic Kernel (`rumelt-strategy-kernel`)

#### 1. What it decomposes in this transcript
For each structural whitespace opportunity identified by Wardley Mapping, Rumelt's Strategic Kernel strips out industry hype, vanity metrics, and prohibitive capital requirements (e.g., competing with Oracle's $38B data center debt or Snap's $1B AR hardware spend) to isolate an asymmetric wedge for a two-person startup.

#### 2. What to look for
- **Diagnosis**: Formulate a sharp, unadorned statement of the critical obstacle preventing incumbents or users from solving the problem.
  - *Example (XR)*: The obstacle is not display hardware fidelity; it is the economic friction of rewriting spatial apps across fragmented runtimes (Android XR vs. VisionOS) without a guaranteed audience.
  - *Example (Synthetic Swarms)*: The obstacle is not model generation capability; it is the inability of platforms and security teams to verify coordinated behavioral intent across thousands of decentralized AI accounts.
  - *Failure mode*: The diagnosis must not simply rephrase the challenge or wish for a better outcome; it must identify the structural choke point.
- **Guiding Policy**: Specify the team's decisive advantage and explicit trade-offs.
  - For a two-person team, this requires choosing software/middleware over hardware, open protocols over proprietary silos, and strictly defining what the team will *not* pursue (e.g., zero custom silicon, zero physical manufacturing, zero direct model pre-training).
  - *Failure mode*: Avoid platitudes ("build the best AI platform") dressed up as policy; ensure the policy defines how resources are concentrated.
- **Coherent Actions**: Define a set of 2 to 3 mutually reinforcing operational commitments where each action magnifies the impact of the others (e.g., building a lightweight open-source abstraction layer that directly channels users into a high-margin proprietary testing/telemetry service).
  - *Failure mode*: Avoid disjointed "to-do lists" or importing ungrounded features not directly tied to overcoming the diagnosed obstacle.

#### 3. What to hand to the next framework
Hand off a focused strategic thesis for each opportunity: a validated Diagnosis of the choke point, a Guiding Policy establishing asymmetric leverage, and a set of Coherent Actions.

### Step 3: PDCA / Deming Cycle (`pdca-deming`)

#### 1. What it decomposes in this transcript
The PDCA framework decomposes the Coherent Actions into an immediate, disciplined 30-day operational protocol that two founders can start executing this month to validate or invalidate the startup thesis with empirical evidence.

#### 2. What to look for
- **Plan (Hypothesis Formulation)**:
  - Articulate a crisp, falsifiable hypothesis linking the specific customer problem to an initial offering.
  - Define clear quantitative baseline targets (e.g., "Out of 40 indie spatial developers contacted, at least 8 will commit to integrating our Android XR compatibility shim within 10 days").
  - *Failure mode*: Do not use unfalsifiable or circular hypotheses; ensure the metric would genuinely disprove the venture if missed.
- **Do (Contained Pilot Execution)**:
  - Scope a sprint strictly deliverable within weeks 1 to 3 by two people.
  - Enforce rapid prototyping: a focused CLI, a targeted landing page with synthetic integration demos, or a lightweight SDK wrapper built on open APIs.
  - *Failure mode*: Prevent scope creep; eliminate uncontained pilots requiring third-party enterprise sign-offs or hardware certifications.
- **Check (Empirical Measurement)**:
  - Establish exact qualitative and quantitative signals to evaluate by Day 25: active usage rates, API error frequencies, qualitative developer feedback, and explicit willingness-to-pay signals.
  - *Failure mode*: Guard against treating "Check" as subjective team impressions or vanity impressions (as mocked in the transcript's 6M views vs. 292 likes example); require concrete behavioral commitments.
- **Act (Standardize or Pivot Decision)**:
  - Formulate unambiguous decision triggers for Day 30:
    - *Standardize*: If adoption thresholds are met, harden the pilot into a public release, automate onboarding, and initiate monetization.
    - *Pivot*: If the core friction does not yield developer traction, alter the wedge or redeploy the architecture to an adjacent whitespace identified during the pilot.
  - *Failure mode*: Avoid ambiguous review outcomes where failure is rationalized away without a decision rule.
