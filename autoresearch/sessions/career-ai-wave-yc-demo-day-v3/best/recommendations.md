## Comparative Path Ranking & Strategic Synthesis

To determine the highest expected-value (EV) trajectory within Ivan's 6-to-12-month runway constraint, all four paths are systematically evaluated and ranked against his specific background (10 years at Google, Staff Engineer at Lyft since October 2025, Brooklyn NYC resident, PyTorch, LangGraph multi-agent systems, GCP pipelines) and his primary gap (zero public footprint):

1. **Path (b): Founding a 1-to-3 Person AI Startup in an Underserved Vertical (RANK 1 — Primary Path, Highest Expected Value)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Vertical B2B AI startups are capturing unprecedented upfront commercial revenue: "dollar value contracts that startups can close in like the first few months of their life are just bigger than anything we've ever seen... big step-function growth" (Harj Taggar, lines 1613–1635). Big Tech incumbents cannot respond because "incumbents can't actually build the products because the engineers that work at these bigger companies don't even believe in AI" (Harj Taggar, lines 1696–1698), while elite frontier lab talent suffers from cultural blindness toward operational back-office friction: "the best people at Open AI or Anthropic are not going to be thrilled to build, like, auditing software or auditing agents" (Harj Taggar, lines 1884–1887) and "The best engineers at Google don't want to build a shopping product... Now they probably want to work on Gemini" (TBPN Host, lines 1905–1909).
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Operating from Brooklyn gives Ivan physical access to dense, high-margin, unsexy operational verticals (fund administration, RIA compliance, legal trusts) where manual workbook audits take hours (Michael at Crunch, lines 4120–4125). Deploying agentic verification sidecars that augment existing spreadsheets bypasses enterprise resistance ("trust rather than tech"; Cole Dermott, lines 5316–5318), de-risking runway within 90 days while preserving 1,000x venture upside (Paul Graham, lines 5988–5990).

2. **Path (d): Maximizing Long-Run Wealth and Leverage (RANK 2 — Evaluated Against Standalone Pre-IPO Equity & Quant Systems)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Evaluated against standalone late-stage pre-IPO unicorns (e.g., Anthropic, Databricks, Anduril) and high-compensation quantitative trading systems. Late-stage private secondary equity suffers severe counterparty risk and illiquidity discounts: "forward contracts are notoriously hard to settle in private companies, and counterparty risk is extremely real" (Matt Grimm, line 1436), with major unicorns explicitly banning secondary transfers ("forward contracts are explicitly disallowed by Anduril's stock plan and bylaws", line 1471). High-compensation W-2 quant roles lack power-law equity compounding: "what's tax evasion going to get you, like 2x returns in a world where getting the right startups will get you a thousand X returns?" (Paul Graham, lines 5988–5990).
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Standalone wealth maximization without early equity ownership yields taxed W-2 wages or illiquid private shares with high counterparty friction. Path (d) is best realized not as a standalone wage job, but as the economic consequence of executing Path (b): launching an operational startup backed by open-source systems gravity creates competing alternatives—a venture funded at standard YC valuations (~$30M; Paul Graham, line 5841) or competing top-tier liquid offers.

3. **Path (a): Staff or Research Engineer Offer at a Major AI Lab (RANK 3 — High-Floor Downside Liquidity Hedge)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Frontier labs operate in an asymmetric "lemons market where it's hard to tell who the good people are beforehand and hiring someone bad is quite costly" (TBPN Host quoting Dwarkesh Patel, lines 1216–1218), while automated resume routing ensures that "nobody actually looks at job applications" (TBPN Host, lines 3518–3519). Furthermore, technical interviewers immediately "sniff out a faker like that" (Paul Graham, line 5765) when candidates showcase superficial prompt wrappers. However, publishing low-level systems code bypasses recruiters entirely: Kareem at Materiel released an open-source codebase that gained 3,600 GitHub stars in 5 weeks, immediately prompting an OpenAI Member of Technical Staff to reach out directly (lines 3770–3774, 3817–3820).
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Maintain Path (a) as a liquid safety floor ($600k–$850k+ total compensation). By packaging his evaluation harness as a low-level systems benchmark rather than a generic prompt wrapper, Ivan turns his open-source work for Path (b) into an active recruiting bat-signal for frontier labs.

4. **Path (c): AI Research Track (Academic Papers, Open Base Models) (RANK 4 — Strictly Eliminated / Unviable)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Foundation text modeling has converged at the frontier: "like the text-based models, like LLMs, there's not an awful lot of competition going on there anymore. Things have sort of converged at the top" (Ben, lines 4676–4679). Pre-training base models requires hundreds of millions of dollars in custom silicon clusters such as AWS Trainium 3 ("four times as fast", reducing operational costs by 50%; lines 156–178) and Google TPU v7 clusters ("meta platforms is in talk with Google to buy billions of dollars worth of advanced AI processors", line 242). Peer review cycles take 12–18 months and generate zero commercial revenue.
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Ivan has "no from-scratch research results yet" and a strict 6-to-12-month liquid runway. Burning personal capital to train models from scratch guarantees complete financial insolvency before publication. Strictly eliminated.

**The Highest-EV Synthesis:** Pursue Path (b) anchored in NYC customer density as the primary engine to capture 1,000x equity upside, while publishing a low-level PyTorch/GCP systems artifact that simultaneously unlocks Path (a) lab offers, transforming Path (d) leverage into a guaranteed financial floor without risking runway insolvency.
