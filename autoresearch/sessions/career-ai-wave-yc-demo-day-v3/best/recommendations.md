# Recommendations

Execute a 12-month dual-asset transition that leverages Staff-level systems engineering to build a defensible vertical AI startup in New York City while generating competing frontier lab offers and protecting liquid runway.

## Comparative Path Ranking & Strategic Synthesis

To determine the highest expected-value (EV) strategy within Ivan's 6-to-12-month runway constraint, all four paths are evaluated and ranked against his technical profile (10 years Google, Staff Engineer at Lyft, Python/PyTorch, LangGraph multi-agent orchestration, GCP pipelines) and his primary gap (zero public footprint):

1. **Path (b): Founding a 1-to-3 Person AI Startup in an Underserved Vertical (RANK 1 — Primary Path, Highest Expected Value)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Startups in late 2025 are securing unprecedented early enterprise revenue: "dollar value contracts that startups can close in like the first few months of their life are just bigger than anything we've ever seen... big step-function growth" (Harj Taggar, lines 1613–1635). Big tech incumbents are paralyzed because "the engineers that work at these bigger companies don't even believe in AI" (Harj Taggar, lines 1696–1702), and frontier lab talent rejects unsexy operational problems: "The best people at OpenAI or Anthropic are not going to be thrilled to build, like, auditing software or auditing agents... The best engineers at Google don't want to build a shopping product" (Harj Taggar, lines 1884–1909).
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Ivan's LangGraph and GCP pipeline capabilities allow him to build mission-critical, error-resilient agentic workflows that augment domain workers. Closing a single $20,000–$50,000 pilot or securing institutional seed backing (~$30M standard YC batch demo day valuation; Paul Graham, line 5841) de-risks his runway within 90 to 180 days while retaining 1,000x venture upside (Paul Graham, lines 5982–5990).

2. **Path (d): Maximizing Long-Run Wealth and Leverage (RANK 2 — Complementary Meta-Strategy)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Power-law tech wealth is generated through equity ownership in scarce infrastructure, not incremental salary bargaining: "The big wins don't come from little cheats that get you 2x multiples in a world of, like, 1,000 X returns" (Paul Graham, lines 5982–5990). Furthermore, investor ecosystems act as concentrated leverage networks ("all these alumni are investors... taken over Silicon Valley", Paul Graham, lines 5938–5948).
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Path (d) is not a standalone operational job; it is the economic consequence of executing Path (b). Shipping public proof-of-work manufactures competing alternatives—a funded venture, an acquisition offer, or competing high-equity Staff offers.

3. **Path (a): Staff or Research Engineer Offer at a Major AI Lab (RANK 3 — Viable Downside Hedge)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Frontier labs face an asymmetric hiring "lemons market" (lines 1216–1218) where inbound technical leads instantly "sniff out a faker like that" (Paul Graham, line 5765). Without a public footprint, Ivan cannot command top-of-market compensation ($600k–$900k+ liquid packages) via blind job boards. However, public open-source artifacts bypass recruiters entirely: Kareem at Materiel released open tooling that achieved 3,600 GitHub stars in 5 weeks, instantly prompting an OpenAI Member of Technical Staff to reach out directly (lines 3770–3774, 3817–3820).
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Maintain Path (a) as a de-risked liquidation floor. The open-source benchmarking code Ivan publishes to validate Path (b) serves simultaneously as the bat-signal to trigger competitive lab interview loops at Day 90.

4. **Path (c): AI Research Track (Papers, Open Models) (RANK 4 — Strictly Unviable / Eliminated)**
   - *Evaluation & Transcript Evidence:* Foundation text models have converged at the frontier: "the text-based models, like LLMs, there's not an awful lot of competition going on there anymore. Things have sort of converged at the top there" (Ben, lines 4676–4679). Training competitive foundation models requires specialized semiconductor clusters running into hundreds of millions of dollars, such as Amazon's custom Trainium 3 (4X speedup, 50% operational cost reduction; lines 156–178) and Google TPU v7 clusters (lines 242–246). Academic review cycles take 12–18 months and yield zero revenue.
   - *Fit for Ivan:* Ivan has "no from-scratch research results yet." Spending a 6-to-12-month liquid runway training models from scratch guarantees personal financial insolvency before peer review completes. Strictly eliminated.

**The Highest-EV Synthesis:** Pursue Path (b) as the primary engine to capture 1,000x startup equity while manufacturing a "Dual-Asset" open-source artifact that simultaneously unlocks Path (a) lab offers, transforming Path (d) leverage into a guaranteed financial floor.

## 90-Day Sequential Action Plan

1. Shed non-critical engineering commitments and internal promotion overhead at Lyft to carve out 20 hours of weekly deep-work runway.

   - Non-obvious: Do not quit Lyft on Day 1. Instead, adopt subtractive focus: stop writing uncredited platform refactoring design docs, step down from cross-functional hiring and promotion committees, and decline exploratory multi-quarter roadmap meetings. Deliver core Staff Lyft duties in 25 hours/week, converting the recovered 20 hours into protected founder deep work without tapping personal savings.

2. Package and open-source a production-grade multi-agent reliability and error-recovery benchmark on GitHub and X.
   - Owner: Ivan (Open Source Author / Architect).
   - First deadline: Day 21 (April 1, 2026).
   - Resource needed: Personal GCP project credits ($100 budget), Python, PyTorch, LangGraph runtime, X and GitHub accounts.
   - Why: Technical hiring and investor vetting in AI operates as an asymmetric "lemons market" where builders instantly "sniff out a faker like that" (Paul Graham, line 5765; transcript lines 1216–1218). 3. Conduct 30 in-person customer discovery interviews with boutique financial, trust, and compliance operators across New York City.

- **The Enterprise Procurement Freeze (The "Bureaucrat" Trap):** An enterprise champion orally agrees to a deployment but routes the contract into a 9-month SOC2, ISO27001, and infosec compliance funnel that drains Ivan's personal runway.
  - *Transcript Hint:* Paul Graham warns that selling big deals to big companies "take a long time and make your product stupider" (line 6162).
  - *Tripwire:* If enterprise security reviews extend past 21 calendar days without an approved security exception or signed paid discovery agreement, immediately pivot the customer to a secure on-premise local runner or walk away to focus on smaller boutique firms.
- **The "Brainrot" Wrapper Degradation Risk:** Building a superficial UI on top of an LLM API that gets vaporized as foundation models release native workflow features.
  - *Transcript Hint:* Sam Altman's rule cited by Harj Taggar: "If your entire business is just predicated on the model not getting better, you're going to have a bad time" (lines 1858–1861). Paul Graham's condemnation of gimmicks like Chad IDE (lines 5552–5576).
  - *Tripwire:* If more than 50% of the product's code consists of prompt engineering rather than proprietary deterministic data ingestion, schema validation, and multi-agent state recovery on GCP, halt feature work immediately and rebuild the domain evaluation engine.
- **The Zero-Inbound Visibility Vacuum:** Publishing technical code without developer distribution, resulting in an ignored repository and zero market signal.
  - *Transcript Hint:* The AI ecosystem operates as an asymmetric "lemons market" where unverified talent is ignored (lines 1216–1218).
  - *Tripwire:* If the open-source repository fails to accumulate at least 150 GitHub stars and 20 forks by Day 25, package the technical teardown into a granular breakdown on X dissecting real-world LangGraph production failures, and cross-post directly to Hacker News and AI infrastructure Discord channels.
