# Notes

## Insights
- **Value Chain Migration**: Value in late 2025 has moved from foundation models and generic agent tooling (commoditizing into utilities) toward vertical agents and full-stack AI-native services (e.g., Fernstone, Saver, Crunched).
- **Enterprise Willingness-to-Pay**: Corporate bureaucrats mandated to "AI-ify" operations cannot get solutions from slow incumbents, creating a window for 1–3 person AI startups to close large step-function contracts in months (Harj Taggar, Paul Graham).
- **Enterprise Deployment Bottleneck**: Fortune 500s cannot deploy un-permissioned LLMs without granular role-based access control and data security (Materiel / Kareem); solving access governance upfront unblocks enterprise pilots.
- **Runway Constraint as Hard Filter**: With a 6–12 month runway, Path (c) (from-scratch research) is eliminated due to long publication latency and zero revenue. Path (b) (vertical startup) offers the highest expected value and quickest solvency.
- **Dual-Use Asset Strategy**: Building a vertical multi-agent system (using LangGraph and GCP strengths) and open-sourcing its deterministic evaluation harness simultaneously drives commercial traction (Path b) and creates public technical proof for inbound lab offers (Path d & a).
- **Prospective Ruin & Hard Kill Gates**: The most common founder trap is quitting salary before validation. Retaining Staff salary at Lyft during Days 1–90 preserves 100% of runway, enforced by a strict Day 90 kill/pivot gate (LOI/pilot $\ge \$25\text{k}$ to resign; else pivot to lab recruiting).
- **Anti-Gimmick Discipline**: High-contract enterprise buyers demand deterministic reliability; founders must avoid novelty rage-bait and developer gimmicks (e.g., Clad Labs / Chad IDE) that Paul Graham warns destroy long-term enterprise credibility.
