2. **Rank 2: Path (a) — Staff Systems or Inference Engineer at a Major AI Lab (Second Highest Expected Value; Primary Hedge)**

3. **Rank 3: Path (d) — Maximizing Long-Run Wealth and Leverage via Inbound Optionality (Third; Downstream Emergent State)**
   - *Analysis:* Securing multiple competing offers and elite advisory leverage cannot be executed as a standalone starting operator. It requires extreme market visibility or proprietary commercial traction. ## Risks and What to Watch

1. **Enterprise Procurement and Security Review Stall**
   - *Transcript Hint:* Kareem from Materiel warns that enterprise deployment requires rigorous security and permissions architecture (*"Fortune 500s can't just unleash LLM... They need to think very concretely about who has secure access"*, lines 3792–3797).
   - *Tripwire:* An enterprise partner agrees in principle to a pilot but enterprise infosec/procurement review remains unassigned or inactive after 21 calendar days.
   - *Mitigation:* Pre-empt infosec stalls by providing a complete role-based access control (RBAC) architecture, customer-managed encryption key support, and zero-data-retention guarantees on Day 1 of discovery.

2. **Incumbent Engineering Sabotage and "Not-Invented-Here" Resistance**
   - *Transcript Hint:* Harj Taggar notes that internal engineers at large incumbents often dismiss external AI solutions or actively resist deployment (*"the incumbents can't actually build the products because the engineers that work at these bigger companies don't even believe in AI"*, lines 1696–1702).
   - *Tripwire:* The enterprise operational buyer introduces internal IT/software teams who request an indefinite pause to evaluate building the orchestration graph in-house.
   - *Mitigation:* Anchor the relationship exclusively to the business unit operational head (the "AI-ify" bureaucrat who owns the P&L and deadline), framing the tool as operational software that requires zero internal IT engineering hours to maintain.

3. **Agent Non-Determinism and Hallucination in Mission-Critical Workflows**
   - *Transcript Hint:* Paul Graham notes that enterprise users have zero patience for non-functional hype (*"It's got to actually work because they don't have time to mess around with things that don't work"*, lines 5518–5520), and Cole Dermott of Locus confirms that the core barrier to autonomous agent adoption is trust (*"that's really the biggest barrier right now is trust rather than tech"*, lines 5316–5318).
   - *Tripwire:* Pilot agent error rate exceeds 2% on ground-truth audit samples or produces an un-flagged discrepancy in a live staging test.
   - *Mitigation:* Implement strict Pydantic structured output validation, multi-model consensus checks for ambiguous fields, and mandatory human-in-the-loop review queues for all confidence scores below 95%.

4. **Moonlighting IP Contamination with Current Employer**
   - *Decomposition Insight:* Big Tech employment agreements strictly assign intellectual property created during employment if it relates to the employer's business or uses company resources.
   - *Tripwire:* Any code commit, customer call, or research artifact touches Lyft equipment, Slack channels, Google Workspace, or relates to ride-sharing, transit dispatch, or mapping.
   - *Mitigation:* Strict physical and temporal air-gapping: 100% personal hardware, personal Wi-Fi/cellular hotspots, off-hours execution, and vertical niches strictly restricted to logistics compliance, corporate finance, or insurance.
